# Ancient Egyptian Royalty and Military, by Era

Reference corpus for Hathor Studio (historically grounded scenes, objects, templates). Every claim carries the
page it was read from. Where sources disagree, both are given. Object ids in `[brackets]` refer to
`egyptian-royalty-military-items.json`; template ids to `egyptian-royalty-military-templates.json`.

Research date: 2026-09-26. Sources are Wikipedia, museum collection pages (Metropolitan Museum of Art,
Grand Egyptian Museum), the Egyptian Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities, a peer-reviewed article abstract
(Kamrin 2009) and a chapter by Bietak & Rendsburg. Some secondary sites (egypt-museum.com, Madain Project,
Ancient Egypt Online) are used only for scene descriptions and are marked as secondary.

## 1. Regalia reference (all eras)

- **Hedjet (White Crown)** — crown of Upper Egypt; worn by pharaohs and by Horus and Nekhbet ([WP Crowns of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowns_of_Egypt)). Worn
  by Scorpion II and Narmer in the Predynastic Period ([WP Pharaoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharaoh)). [hedjet-white-crown]
- **Deshret (Red Crown)** — crown of Lower Egypt; worn by pharaohs and by Horus, Wadjet and Neith ([WP Crowns of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowns_of_Egypt));
  "dates back to pre-dynastic times" ([WP Pharaoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharaoh)). [deshret-red-crown]
- **Pschent (Double Crown)** — red and white crowns combined, with uraeus and vulture ([WP Crowns of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowns_of_Egypt)); "first
  documented in the middle of the First Dynasty" ([WP Pharaoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharaoh)). [pschent-double-crown]
- **Khepresh (Blue Crown)** — New Kingdom; worn "in battle and ceremonies"; with uraeus ([WP Crowns of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowns_of_Egypt)). The Met
  calls it apparently Amenhotep III's preferred headdress ([Met 56.138 Amenhotep III in the Blue Crown](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/544477)). [khepresh-blue-crown]
- **Nemes** — striped linen headcloth with two lappets falling in front of the shoulders ([WP Nemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemes)). Sources
  differ on its first appearance: "dating from Djoser's reign" ([WP Pharaoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharaoh)); but the MacGregor Label of the 1st
  Dynasty king Den is described as "the first complete depiction of an Egyptian king with the so-called nemes"
  ([WP Den](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Den_(pharaoh))). [nemes-headcloth]
- **Khat** — head cloth worn by Hatshepsut, "topped with the uraeus" ([WP Hatshepsut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatshepsut)). [khat-headcloth]
- **Atef** — hedjet with ostrich feathers, sometimes uraeus and ram horns; worn by Osiris and other gods
  ([WP Crowns of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowns_of_Egypt)). [atef-crown]
- **Hemhem** — hedjet, uraeus and ram horns; worn by non-Egyptian rulers and certain gods ([WP Crowns of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowns_of_Egypt)).
  [hemhem-crown]
- **Cap crown** — gold band, linen skullcap, ribbon bow; attested from the Old Kingdom; associated with the
  Kushite 25th Dynasty ([WP Crowns of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowns_of_Egypt)). See §8 for the Kushite form. [kushite-cap-crown]
- **Vulture crown** — "a vulture draped over the head, with its wings hanging down on the sides"; associated
  with Nekhbet; worn by queens regularly from the Fifth Dynasty (Khentkaus II among the earliest); in the New
  Kingdom the vulture's head was increasingly replaced by a uraeus ([WP Vulture crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulture_crown)). [vulture-crown]
- **Modius** — "flat-topped cylindrical headdress"; Meritamen (daughter of Ramesses II) wears one "decorated
  with uraei" ([WP Modius (headdress)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modius_(headdress))). [modius-crown]
- **Uraeus** — upright cobra of Wadjet; paired with Nekhbet's vulture as the "Two Ladies" ([WP Uraeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uraeus)). The gold
  uraeus of Senusret II (El-Lahun, 1919) is 6.7 cm with lapis lazuli head, carnelian and turquoise inlays and
  granite eyes ([WP Uraeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uraeus)). [uraeus-senusret-ii]
- **Crook and flail** — earliest crook from Naqada II tomb U547, Abydos; paired by the Second Dynasty; the only
  surviving royal examples are Tutankhamun's: bronze staffs with alternating stripes of blue glass, obsidian and
  gold; flail beads of gilded wood ([WP Crook and flail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crook_and_flail)). [crook-and-flail]
- **False beard** — Tutankhamun's mask beard weighs 2.5 kg, blue glass inlay in a plaited pattern ([WP Mask of Tutankhamun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mask_of_Tutankhamun));
  Hatshepsut is shown with kilt and false beard ([Met 30.3.1 Kneeling statue of Hatshepsut](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/544448)) ([WP Hatshepsut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatshepsut)); Khafre's face is identified in part by "traces
  of a royal beard" ([Met 26.7.1392 Face of Khafre](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/543896)). [royal-false-beard]
- **Shendyt** — linen kilt "typically extending to above the knees", worn by pharaohs, gods and commoners,
  adopted by soldiers ([WP Shendyt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shendyt)). [shendyt-kilt]
- **Bull's tail** — attached to Narmer's belt on the Narmer Palette ([WP Narmer Palette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmer_Palette)). [bulls-tail]
- **Usekh / wesekh broad collar** — curved rows of tubular/teardrop beads (faience, glass, stone, metal) with
  terminals and sometimes a counterweight; from the Old Kingdom on ([WP Usekh collar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usekh_collar)). [usekh-broad-collar]
- **Nine Bows** — generic enemies of Egypt; placed under the king's feet; Tutankhamun's footstool and sandals
  carry them ([WP Nine Bows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Bows)). [nine-bows-footstool] [nine-bows-sandals]
- **Tutankhamun's Golden Throne** — wood covered with chased gold sheet, inlaid with glass, faience and
  semi-precious stones; backrest shows "the king and queen under a floral canopy pierced by the rays of the Aten
  sun disk"; winged uraei hold the king's cartouche on the sides; H 103.5 × W 59 × L 73 cm ([Grand Egyptian Museum, Golden Throne](https://gem.eg/en/collection/artefacts/golden-throne)).
  [golden-throne-tutankhamun]

## 2. Early Dynastic (c. 3150 BC –)

**Royalty.** The Narmer Palette (siltstone, c. 63 × 42 cm, c. 3200–3000 BC, Hierakonpolis, Cairo CG 14716 /
JE 32169) shows Narmer in the White Crown on one side and the Red Crown on the other — "the earliest known
example of a king wearing both types of headdress" — with a bull's tail, standard-bearers before him, serpopards
and decapitated enemies ([WP Narmer Palette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmer_Palette)). [narmer-palette] Den is shown smiting an enemy of "Asian origin" (braids,
beard) with a mace on the MacGregor Label; his records list "smiting of the Asiatic people" and troops sent to
Sinai ([WP Den](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Den_(pharaoh))). Pschent first documented mid-1st Dynasty ([WP Pharaoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharaoh)).

**Military.** Maces: disc maces c. 3850–3650 BCE; the pear-shaped (piriform) head replaced them in Naqada II
(3600–3250 BCE) and continued through Naqada III ([WP Mace (bludgeon)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mace_(bludgeon))). Nine Bows earliest instances late predynastic
(3200–3000 BCE) at Hierakonpolis ([WP Nine Bows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Bows)). Neith worship attested Naqada II (c. 3600–3350 BC) and important
in the Early Dynastic Period ([WP Neith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neith)). [piriform-mace-head] [disc-mace-head]

## 3. Old Kingdom (2686–2181 BC)

**Royalty.** Nemes becomes the most common royal headgear "from Djoser's reign" ([WP Pharaoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharaoh)). Khafre (Dyn. 4,
ca. 2520–2494 BCE) built the Second Pyramid and the Great Sphinx; statues in fine stones; a travertine face in
the Met carries "traces of a royal beard" ([Met 26.7.1392 Face of Khafre](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/543896)). Vulture crown worn by queens from the Fifth Dynasty ([WP Vulture crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulture_crown)).

**Military.** Nome governors raised volunteer armies; Djoser began recruiting annually; weapons were shields,
spears, cudgels, maces, daggers and bows — the bow most common, a single-arched bow ([WP Military of ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_ancient_Egypt)). Forts on the Delta
borders, in the Eastern Desert and in Nubia ([WP Military of ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_ancient_Egypt)). Weni, under Pepi I, "included Nubian mercenaries in the army
for the first time" and fought the "sand-dwellers" at least five times ([WP Weni the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weni_the_Elder)). The Medjay are first documented
under Pepi I ([WP Medjay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medjay)). Troops were moved by ship from the Late Old Kingdom ([WP Military of ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_ancient_Egypt)).

## 4. Middle Kingdom (to 1650 BC)

**Royalty.** Senusret III (c. 1878–1839 BCE) is shown with a weathered, heavy-lidded, furrowed face and large
ears over an idealised body ([WP Senusret III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senusret_III)) ([Met 26.7.1394 Face of Senwosret III](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/544184)). Montu was a supreme god in the Middle Kingdom; the 11th-Dynasty kings
named Mentuhotep ("Montu is satisfied") championed him ([WP Montu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montu)). Gold uraeus of Senusret II ([WP Uraeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uraeus)).

**Military.** Standing armies kept from the 12th Dynasty ([WP Military of ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_ancient_Egypt)). Mentuhotep II campaigned to the Second
Cataract ([WP Military of ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_ancient_Egypt)). Senusret III campaigned in Nubia in years 8, 10, 16 and 19, built forts at Buhen, Semna,
Shalfak and Uronarti, and erected the Semna boundary stela ([WP Senusret III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senusret_III)); the Met notes 12th-Dynasty fortresses at the
Second Cataract to secure gold and hold back Kush, and that Nubian archers served as mercenaries ([Met essay, The Land of Nubia](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/nubia)).
Wooden soldier models from Mesehti's tomb at Asyut (11th Dyn., c. 2000 BCE): Egyptian spearmen "equipped with a
hide shield and a white skirt" and 40 Nubian archers, "darker-skinned and wearing a red loincloth" ([WP Mesehti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesehti)).
[egyptian-hide-shield] Model shields from Asyut are painted to imitate cowhide with a rawhide edge (Met 17.9.3,
H 71.5 cm) ([Met 17.9.3 Model shield](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/546270)); shields were "commonly rectangular", cowhide or oxhide over a wooden frame, tapering to a
curved top ([Grand Egyptian Museum, Model Shield of Nakhti](https://gem.eg/en/collection/artefacts/model-shield-of-nakhti)). Duckbill axes (socketed, narrow blade) are a Syro-Palestinian MB IIA type; one very like
the Met's was found in a late 12th-Dynasty tomb at Tell el-Daba (Avaris) ([Met 41.6.9 Duckbill ax head](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/545716)). [duckbill-axe] The epsilon axe (bronze/copper head lashed to the haft at three points) appears in Egyptian art carried with a
shield; historians have called it the "poor man's khopesh" ([WP Epsilon axe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epsilon_axe)). [epsilon-axe]

**Fortress of Buhen.** Sources disagree on the builder: "Under Senusret I, Egyptian armies built a border fort
at Buhen" ([WP Military of ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_ancient_Egypt)); "probably constructed during the rule of Senusret III in around 1860 BCE" ([WP Buhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buhen)). Over 150 m
along the west bank below the Second Cataract, 13,000 m², stone walls ~5 m thick and ~10 m high, a 3 m moat,
drawbridges, bastions, loopholes and a catapult; now under Lake Nasser ([WP Buhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buhen)). [buhen-fortress]

**The Aamu of Shu at Beni Hasan (tomb BH3 of Khnumhotep II).** North wall, third register ([Bietak & Rendsburg, 'Egypt and the Exodus' (PDF)](https://jewishstudies.rutgers.edu/images/documents/faculty/Rendsburg/ch.%202%20text%20%20notes.pdf)). Date —

([Bietak & Rendsburg, 'Egypt and the Exodus' (PDF)](https://jewishstudies.rutgers.edu/images/documents/faculty/Rendsburg/ch.%202%20text%20%20notes.pdf)); Met facsimile dated ca. 1887–1878 BC, reign of Senwosret II ([Met 33.8.17 Leaders of the Aamu of Shu](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/544548)). ## 5. Second Intermediate Period and the Hyksos (1650–1550 BC)

**Who.** 15th Dynasty rulers from the Levant; name from *ḥqꜣ-ḫꜣswt*, "rulers of foreign lands"; Western
Semitic speakers, "may be called for convenience sake Canaanites"; Asiatic presence at Avaris (Tell el-Dab'a)
for 150+ years before Hyksos rule, from c. 1800 BC; direct control likely limited to the eastern Delta ([WP Hyksos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyksos)). Rulers adopted full Egyptian royal titulary and reinscribed seized Middle Kingdom sphinxes ([WP Hyksos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyksos)). An
official is shown with the "mushroom-headed" hairstyle seen at Beni Hasan ([WP Hyksos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyksos)). Palaces and temples at Avaris
resemble northern Syrian and Mesopotamian buildings ([Bietak & Rendsburg, 'Egypt and the Exodus' (PDF)](https://jewishstudies.rutgers.edu/images/documents/faculty/Rendsburg/ch.%202%20text%20%20notes.pdf)). Burials: infants in Canaanite amphorae; horse and
equid burials ([WP Hyksos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyksos)).

**Rulers.** Khyan (throne name Seuserenre), second half of the 17th c. BC by most estimates (range c.
1700–1580 BC); objects with his name found at Knossos, Boğazköy and Baghdad; Bietak sees his reign as the peak
of Hyksos power ([WP Khyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyan)). Apepi (c. 1575–1540 BCE, c. 40 years; Rhind Papyrus dated to his Year 33) worshipped
Seth ([WP Apepi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apepi)); Apepi and Khamudi were the last two kings ([WP Hyksos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyksos)). The Hyksos took Set as patron, equated with
Hadad ([WP Set (deity)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_(deity))).

**What they introduced — disputed.** "The Hyksos have been credited with bringing to Egypt the horse, the
Ourarit (chariot), and the composite bow" ([WP Military of ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_ancient_Egypt)). Against this: "no archaeological, pictorial, or textual evidence
exists that the Hyksos possessed chariots"; attribution of composite bow and sickle-sword is "debatable, with
scholarly opinion currently divided"; they "may have worn full-body armor, whereas the Egyptians did not wear
armor or helmets until the New Kingdom" ([WP Hyksos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyksos)). Bietak & Rendsburg describe the Hyksos society as marked by
"the importation of horses and chariots into Egypt" ([Bietak & Rendsburg, 'Egypt and the Exodus' (PDF)](https://jewishstudies.rutgers.edu/images/documents/faculty/Rendsburg/ch.%202%20text%20%20notes.pdf)). Chariot arrival dates differ: c. 1700 BCE ([WP Egyptian chariot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_chariot));
around 1650 BC ([WP Chariot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chariot)). The khopesh article says it was introduced in this period with the Hyksos ([WP Khopesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khopesh));
the Met calls the sickle sword "newly imported from western Asia" in the New Kingdom ([Met 21.2.61 Model of the Yuya & Tjuyu chariot](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/571749)).

**Theban war and expulsion.** Seqenenre Tao (c. 1560–1555 BC) fought Apepi; a 2021 CT study found his facial
fractures "correlated well with the Hyksos weapons" ([WP Seqenenre Tao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seqenenre_Tao)). Kamose (c. 1555–1550 BC): Medjay overran the
garrison at Nefrusy; he intercepted Apepi's letter to the ruler of Kush proposing a pincer ([WP Kamose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamose)); "the first
Egyptian ruler to use the chariot and cavalry units in battle" per ([WP Egyptian chariot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_chariot)) (single source). Kerma invaded deep
into Egypt c. 1575–1550 BC ([WP Kerma culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerma_culture)). Medjay served under Kamose ([WP Medjay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medjay)). Ahmose I took Tjaru, then Avaris
(regnal year 18–19), then besieged Sharuhen for three years; Ahmose son of Ebana served in the fleet ([WP Ahmose I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmose_I))
and "fought in the canal against Pezedku of Avaris" ([WP Ancient Egyptian navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_navy)). Excavation suggests Avaris "was abandoned by the
Hyksos" rather than destroyed; Ahmose's victory c. 1540 BC ([WP Hyksos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyksos)). Ahmose's reign dates range 1570–1546 to
1539–1514 BC depending on chronology ([WP Ahmose I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmose_I)). Queen Ahhotep (I) was regent; a Karnak stela says she "united
its officer class"; whether she commanded troops is debated ([WP Ahhotep I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahhotep_I)). Ahhotep II burial: ceremonial axe of Ahmose
(copper, gold, electrum, wood; Minoan-style griffin; CG 52645/JE 4673, Luxor Museum), three golden flies
(military awards), lapis bracelets with Ahmose's name, an usekh collar, lion armbands, gold and silver model

## 7. New Kingdom — 19th (Ramesside) and 20th Dynasties

**Royalty.** Seti I means "man of Set"; Ramesses I came from a military family at Avaris; Ramesses II set up the
"Year 400 Stela" ([WP Set (deity)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_(deity))). Ramesses II reigned 1279–1213 BC; capital Pi-Ramesses ([WP Ramesses II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_II)). Nefertari wore the vulture
crown ([WP Vulture crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulture_crown)) ([WP Crowns of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowns_of_Egypt)); Abu Simbel's small temple honours her, built c. 1264–1244 BC ([WP Abu Simbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Simbel)).

**Kadesh (May 1274 BC, Year 5).** Divisions: "Amun, Re, Set, and the apparently newly-formed Ptah"; 16,000
infantry, 2,000 chariots, 4,000 charioteers; Ne'arin troops (possibly Canaanite mercenaries or Egyptians)
relieved the camp; Sherden appear for the first time as Egyptian mercenaries; lighter Egyptian chariots; outcome
a stalemate; accounts at Abydos, Luxor, Karnak, Abu Simbel and the Ramesseum ([WP Battle of Kadesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kadesh)). The general article
describes four armies "named after the Egyptian gods Ra, Amen, Ptah, and Sutekh" ([WP Military of ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_ancient_Egypt)). Abu Simbel shows the
king in his chariot shooting at fleeing enemies ([WP Abu Simbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Simbel)). Template: `chariot-charge-division`.

**Sherden/Shardana.** Mentioned in the Amarna Letters c. 1350 BC; defeated by Ramesses II (Year 2 ([WP Ramesses II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_II)); "1278
BC" ([WP Sherden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherden))); many then in the royal bodyguard, "conspicuous by their horned helmets" ([WP Ramesses II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_II)). Reliefs show
horned helmets with a disc at the crest, round shields, long swords (possibly Naue II), kilts and corslets;
origin debated (Ionia vs. Nuragic Sardinia) ([WP Sherden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherden)). [sherden-horned-helmet] [sherden-round-shield]
[sherden-long-sword] Template: `shardana-bodyguard`.

**The Hittite bride.** Maathorneferure, daughter of Hattusili III and Puduhepa, married Ramesses II in his Year 34

**Merneptah.** Defeated a Libyan-led confederacy at Perire c. 1200 BC ([WP Sea Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Peoples)); "9100 swords of the Meshwesh

**Ramesses III (c. 1185–1154 BC, 20th Dyn.).** Libyan wars Year 5 and Year 11; Year 8 Sea Peoples (Peleset,

## 8. Third Intermediate Period (ca. 1070–664 BC) and Libyans

22nd (Bubastite) Dynasty, 947–713 BC, founded by Shoshenq I of the Meshwesh; capitals Bubastis and Tanis ([WP 22nd Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-second_Dynasty_of_Egypt)). Meshwesh first noted under Amenhotep III supplying cattle; Egyptian art shows them in antelope-hide

## 9. 25th Dynasty — the Kushites

**Background: Kerma.** Kerma culture c. 2500–1500 BC, peak 1750–1500 BC; Western Deffufa c. 1750 BC; its army
"notably composed of archers"; invaded Egypt 1575–1550 BC; destroyed by Thutmose I c. 1504 BC ([WP Kerma culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerma_culture)). Kush
phases: Napatan 656–270 BC, Meroitic 270 BC–4th c. AD ([WP Kingdom of Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kush)).

**Kings.** Piye (c. 747–714 BC ([WP 25th Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fifth_Dynasty_of_Egypt)); 744–714 BC ([WP Piye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piye)); invaded Lower Egypt around 727 BC ([WP Kingdom of Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kush))), Shebitku,
Shabaka, Taharqa (690–664 BC), Tantamani (664–656 BC) ([WP 25th Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fifth_Dynasty_of_Egypt)). The Shabaka/Shebitku order is debated; newer
evidence puts Shebitku first (Shebitku 714–705, Shabaka 705–690) ([WP 25th Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fifth_Dynasty_of_Egypt)) ([WP Shabaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabaka)). Kashta and Alara are not detailed
in the pages read.

**Iconography.** "Kushite kings wore distinctive regalia, including a cap crown, double uraei, and ram's-head
amulets"; on the Met's kneeling king the cap crown has "a falcon engraved in the back, the bandeau with
streamers, and the diadem with many tiny uraei"; a later Saite king recut the double uraeus to one and hammered
out the ram amulets; broad shoulders and narrow waist copy Old Kingdom models ([Met 2002.8 Kneeling Kushite king](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/545027)). Ram amulet worn "tied
around the neck on a thick cord, the ends of which fall forward over the shoulders", sometimes with smaller ram
heads at the ends; rams = Amun, especially revered in Nubia (gold, H 4.2 cm) ([Met 1989.281.98 Ram's-head amulet](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/547771)). Taharqa: "a
double-uraeus skullcap", striding with mekes staff and pleated kilt ([WP Taharqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taharqa)); Sphinx of Taharqo (granite gneiss,
c. 680 BC, Kawa, BM EA 1770) with "two uraei, the Nubian symbol of kingship" ([WP Sphinx of Taharqo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphinx_of_Taharqo)); ram of Amun sheltering
Taharqa (granitic gneiss, c. 683 BCE, Kawa) ([WP Ram statues protecting Taharqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statues_of_Amun_in_the_form_of_a_ram_protecting_King_Taharqa)). Shabaka moved the capital to Memphis ([WP 25th Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fifth_Dynasty_of_Egypt)). Kushite kings
ruled largely from Memphis but were buried in pyramids in Nubia ([Met essay, The Land of Nubia](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/nubia)); pyramids at El-Kurru and Nuri ([WP 25th Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fifth_Dynasty_of_Egypt));
Taharqa's at Nuri is ~52 m square with >1070 shabtis ([WP Taharqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taharqa)). [kushite-cap-crown] [kushite-ram-head-amulet]

**Piye's campaign and stela.** Dark-grey granite, ~6 ft × 4 ft 7 in × 1 ft 5 in, ~2.25 t; found 1862 in the
Amun temple at Jebel Barkal; Egyptian Museum Cairo JE 48862; Year 21 of Piye; battles at Hermopolis and
Memphis; Tefnakht of Sais submits; Piye as "a lover of horses" ([WP Stele of Piye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stele_of_Piye)). Lunette: Piye receiving tribute from
Delta rulers (four, per ([WP Piye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piye))); Nimlot of Hermopolis defected then submitted ([WP Piye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piye)). Piye's four favourite
horses were buried at el-Kurru ([WP Piye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piye)). Template: `piye-victory-campaign`.

**Assyria.** Taharqa beat the Assyrians in 674 BC "according to Babylonian records" ([WP Taharqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taharqa)); the Assyrian-conquest
article dates the Ashkelon defeat to 673 BCE ([WP Assyrian conquest of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_conquest_of_Egypt)). Esarhaddon took Memphis in 671 BC and the royal family as
hostages ([WP Taharqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taharqa)) ([WP Assyrian conquest of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_conquest_of_Egypt)); Ashurbanipal beat Taharqa (667) and Tantamani and sacked Thebes (663) ([WP Assyrian conquest of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_conquest_of_Egypt)). Psamtik I
took Thebes 656 BC ([WP 25th Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fifth_Dynasty_of_Egypt)).

**Meroe.** Capital between the Fifth and Sixth Cataracts ([WP Kingdom of Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kush)). In 24 BCE a Meroitic force reached Thebes;
Petronius sacked Napata and defeated the kandake Amanirenas; Treaty of Samos 20 BCE ([Met essay, The Land of Nubia](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/nubia)). Apedemak, lion-
headed war god with bow and arrow; Lion Temple at Musawwarat es-Sufra (late 3rd c. BCE), Naqa (c. 100–50 BCE) ([WP Apedemak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apedemak)).

## 10. Late Period: Saite 26th Dynasty, Persian 27th/31st

26th Dynasty 664–525 BC at Sais; Psamtik I got Carian and Greek mercenaries via Gyges of Lydia; archaizing art;
devotion to Neith of Sais ([WP 26th Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-sixth_Dynasty_of_Egypt)). Neith: red crown, bow and arrows, emblem of "crossed bows over a shield",
also the sign of Sais ([WP Neith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neith)). [neith-crossed-bows-shield-emblem] Mounted troops and iron weapons came into use
in the Late Period ([WP Military of ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_ancient_Egypt)). Statues of the Napatan king Senkamanisken were buried or hidden at Jebel Barkal,
"presumably due to Psamtik II's attack on Kush in 592 BC" ([WP Senkamanisken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senkamanisken)). Cambyses II won at Pelusium 525
BC; Persian kings took pharaonic titles; a limestone statue of Darius was found at Susa ([WP 27th Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-seventh_Dynasty_of_Egypt)).

## 11. Ptolemaic (305–30 BCE)

Army "very much Macedonian in style", sarissa pike in deep phalanx; land grants to soldiers; by Caesar's time
"a highly disorganized assemblage of mercenaries" ([WP Ptolemaic army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_army)); "the main military force became the infantry
phalanx" ([WP Military of ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_ancient_Egypt)). Raphia, 22 June 217 BC: Ptolemy IV fielded "20,000 Egyptians ... trained in the Macedonian way"
and 73 African elephants against Antiochus III's 102 Indian elephants; Egyptian confidence fed later revolts;
Raphia Decree in Greek and Egyptian ([WP Battle of Raphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Raphia)). Ptolemy VI is shown in the pschent ([WP Crowns of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowns_of_Egypt)). Template:
`raphia-egyptian-phalanx`.

## 12. Roman Egypt and the Fayum portraits

Rome took Egypt in 30 BCE ([Met essay, The Land of Nubia](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/nubia)). Fayum mummy portraits: late 1st c. BC to mid-3rd c. AD; encaustic (wax) with
vivid colour and broad strokes, or tempera; on imported oak, cedar, cypress panels or on the wrappings; ~900
survive; frontal head or head-and-chest; women in chitons and cloaks with gold jewellery, men in tunics with
bands; concentrated at Hawara and Antinoopolis ([WP Fayum mummy portraits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayum_mummy_portraits)). Population: they "reflect the complex synthesis of the
predominant Egyptian culture and that of the elite Greek minority"; subjects include descendants of Greek
mercenaries and native Egyptians, many with Greek or Latin names; a DNA study showed genetic continuity across
pre-Ptolemaic, Ptolemaic and Roman Egypt; the article concludes "those depicted were native Egyptians, who had
adopted the dominant Greco-Roman culture" ([WP Fayum mummy portraits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayum_mummy_portraits)). Template: `fayum-encaustic-portrait`.

## 14. How each people is distinguished in Egyptian art

- **Egyptians.** Men clean-shaven and, at Beni Hasan, barefoot ([Bietak & Rendsburg, 'Egypt and the Exodus' (PDF)](https://jewishstudies.rutgers.edu/images/documents/faculty/Rendsburg/ch.%202%20text%20%20notes.pdf)); spearmen in white kilt with hide
  shield ([WP Mesehti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesehti)); shendyt ([WP Shendyt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shendyt)). The Book of Gates labels them Remetu ([WP Book of Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Gates)) (Reth in ([Ancient Egypt Online, Book of Gates Hour Five](https://ancientegyptonline.co.uk/bookgates5/))). The general
  red-brown-men / yellow-women skin convention was **not** confirmed by a page read in this session.
- **Nubians / Kushites (Nehesy/Nehesu).** Book of Gates: "consistently beardless", "jet black skin and a thick
  red sash across the chest" ([WP Book of Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Gates)); secondary: white garments with red sashes ([egypt-museum.com, Four Races (Seti I)](https://egypt-museum.com/table-of-nations/)). [nubian-red-sash] Mesehti's archers:
  darker-skinned, red loincloth, bows ([WP Mesehti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesehti)). At Huy: feathered headdresses, large hoop earrings, red caps,
  or Egyptianised linen ([egypt-museum.com, Tribute from the South (Huy)](https://egypt-museum.com/tribute-from-the-south-lower-nubia-wawat-upper-nubia-kush/)). Kushite royal: cap crown, double uraeus, ram amulets on a cord ([Met 2002.8 Kneeling Kushite king](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/545027)) ([Met 1989.281.98 Ram's-head amulet](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/547771)).
- **Libyans (Tjemehu/Themehu, Meshwesh, Libu).** Book of Gates: side locks, "open gown-like garments with exposed
  shoulders" ([WP Book of Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Gates)); "fair-skinned" ([Ancient Egypt Online, Book of Gates Hour Five](https://ancientegyptonline.co.uk/bookgates5/)); secondary: patterned robes, feathered headdresses, tattoos ([egypt-museum.com, Four Races (Seti I)](https://egypt-museum.com/table-of-nations/)).
  Meshwesh: antelope-hide cloaks, feathered headdresses, tattoos; bows, spears, daggers ([WP Meshwesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meshwesh)).
- **Asiatics (Aamu; Hyksos; Canaanites).** Book of Gates: bearded, "cloth headbands with hanging excess pieces"
  ([WP Book of Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Gates)). Beni Hasan: mushroom-shaped coiffure, beards, multicoloured garments, relatively fair skin, sandals and
  leather shoes; bow, quiver, spears, duckbill axe ([Bietak & Rendsburg, 'Egypt and the Exodus' (PDF)](https://jewishstudies.rutgers.edu/images/documents/faculty/Rendsburg/ch.%202%20text%20%20notes.pdf)); yellow skin by convention (secondary) ([Madain Project, Procession of the Aamu](https://madainproject.com/procession_of_the_aamu)). Den's
  label: braids and beard ([WP Den](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Den_(pharaoh))).
- **Sea Peoples.** Sherden: horned helmets with a crest disc, round shields, long swords, kilts, corslets
  ([WP Sherden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherden)). Peleset: "feathered" plume helmets, probably leather strips ([WP Peleset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peleset)); with ox-carts, women and
  children ([WP Sea Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Peoples)) ([WP Medinet Habu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medinet_Habu)).
- **The Four Peoples scene.** Book of Gates, Fifth Hour (fourth division), lower register: sixteen men in four
  sets — Egyptians, Asiatics, Nubians, Libyans — led by Horus; "all people are welcome and must make the journey
  to the afterworld"; earliest (incomplete) Book of Gates in Horemheb's tomb; the scene is in the tombs of Seti I,
  Merenptah and Ramesses III ([WP Book of Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Gates)). Horus leans on a staff watching them ([Ancient Egypt Online, Book of Gates Hour Five](https://ancientegyptonline.co.uk/bookgates5/)). Seti I's tomb KV17 was found by
  Belzoni on 16 Oct 1817 and is 137.19 m long ([WP KV17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KV17)); an 1820 hand-coloured lithograph (Hullmandel) copies the
  scene (secondary) ([egypt-museum.com, Four Races (Seti I)](https://egypt-museum.com/table-of-nations/)). Template: `four-peoples-book-of-gates`.
