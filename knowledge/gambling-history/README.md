# Gambling History & Culture — Corpus Shelf

Neutral, education-first reference articles on the **history and culture of lotteries and
gambling**. This shelf is the historical/cultural companion to the **Gambling Education
Center** (odds, EV, house edge, responsible-gambling help) and prime long-form SEO/traffic
content for the Center and the mega-forum.

## What this shelf is (and is not)

- **It is** faithful history and cultural reference: the numbers/policy game and its community
  economics, the frontier gambler-lawmen, the state-lottery transition, Spanish-Mexican and
  ancient gaming traditions, and the modern lottery-integrity scandals — plus a plain reference
  on how **legal** lottery pools/syndicates actually work.
- **It is not** a how-to. Nothing here is a manual for running an illegal numbers bank, a policy
  shop, or a real-money lottery-pooling business. Where the history describes how illegal
  operations were structured, it does so as **documented social history**, the same way any
  history of Prohibition describes bootlegging — description, not instruction.
- Every gambling-facing article carries a **responsible-gambling** footer (education/harm-reduction
  posture, we never take a wager). See the Gambling Education Center for the odds/EV tools and the
  full help directory (1-800-GAMBLER / NCPG / Gamblers Anonymous / GamCare / state helplines).

## Provenance

Drafted from an operator-supplied source document, *"Lotteries and Gambling"* (100pp), which
combined (1) a gambling-history/culture survey and (2) a private content-marketing plan. **Only
the history/culture material is folded into this public corpus.** The marketing plan, and every
private business name and individual consultant named in it, are kept out of public-facing
articles (captured internally instead). Historical public figures (Benny Binion, Ben Thompson,
Doc Holliday, the Maceo brothers, Mickey Cohen, Anthony Cornero, Billy the Kid, Don Clemente
Jacques, etc.) are treated as the public-domain historical record they are, and cross-checked
against general historical knowledge; dates and figures from the source are reproduced as the
source's claims. Treat specific dollar amounts, dates, and statistics as **as-reported** pending
a second source.
