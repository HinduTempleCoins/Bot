# Your first post — an introduction

*Tier A · Graphene-only · stage 1 (intro_post)*

There is an old courtesy in every gathering worth joining: you say who you are.
Not a résumé — a hello. On this chain, your first post is that hello, written
once and kept forever on a public ledger. Let us write it together.

## What you'll learn

- What a top-level post is on a Graphene chain
- How a post is composed: a title, a body, and tags
- Why the *introduceyourself* tag opens the door
- How a post becomes permanent, and what that means

## Step by step

1. **Open the editor in the condenser.** Choose to write a new post (not a
   comment — a comment is a reply to something; a post stands on its own).

2. **Give it a title.** Something true and plain. "Hello — I'm new here, and a
   little about me" does more than any clever line.

3. **Write the body.** Tell the place who you are. What drew you here, what you
   care about, what you might write about. A few real paragraphs are worth more
   than many empty ones — aim for enough that a reader feels they have met
   someone. (The first stage of this little journey looks for an introduction of
   some substance, so let yourself say a true thing or two.)

4. **Add your tags.** Tags are how others find your work. Add **introduceyourself**
   (or *introduction*) as one of them — it is the tag the community watches for
   newcomers, and it is how the place comes to greet you. Add a few more tags for
   the things you actually wrote about.

5. **Publish.** When you post, your **posting key** signs the operation. If you
   are signing through the condenser, you will not be pasting that key into a
   stranger's box — you authorize the action through the chain's own signer. A
   post is a `comment` operation under the hood; on Graphene, a top-level post
   and a reply are the same kind of operation, distinguished by whether they have
   a parent.

6. **Know that it is now permanent.** The text is written into a block. You can
   *edit* a post (an edit is simply a new version of the same `comment`), but the
   history remains on the ledger. Write as someone speaking on the record —
   because you are.

## You did it

You have introduced yourself to a place that will remember it. Somewhere, that
post is now part of the chain's permanent record — your hello, kept. I will read
introductions as they arrive, and when I find yours I will likely say hello back,
and lift it a little, because a newcomer's first word deserves a welcome. The
next step is to become a reader yourself — to go out and meet others' work.
