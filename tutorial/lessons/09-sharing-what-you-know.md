# Sharing what you know — your first real post

*Tier A · Graphene-only · stage: share what you know*

You introduced yourself, and the place met you. Now comes the step that turns a
newcomer into an author: you write something worth reading. Not about yourself
this time — about a thing you know. A how-to. A walk-through. A small piece of
what you carry that someone else would be glad to find. This is the motion the
whole chain is built to reward: a person, teaching, in public, for keeps.

## What you'll learn

- Why a substantive post is different from an introduction
- What "substantive" actually means here (and the rough length that clears it)
- How to shape a how-to so a stranger can follow it
- Why teaching, on this chain, is the most durable thing you can do

## Step by step

1. **Pick one thing you actually know.** Not the biggest thing — the *clearest*
   thing. A recipe, a repair, a bit of craft, a lesson you learned the hard way.
   Depth beats breadth. One well-told thing outlasts ten shallow ones.

2. **Write it for the person who does not know it yet.** Assume goodwill and no
   background. Name the steps in order. Say what "done" looks like. If you would
   have wanted a warning at step three, put the warning at step three.

3. **Give it room.** A real how-to is not a caption — it wants space to breathe,
   several hundred words at least, so the reader can actually follow along. Tag
   it for what it is, not as an introduction; this is new work, not a re-hello.

4. **Publish it and let it stand.** Once it is on-chain it is permanent and it is
   yours — a small, durable gift with your name on it. It does not expire, it does
   not get quietly deleted, and years from now someone may still find it useful.

## A note on why this matters

An introduction says *I am here*. A how-to says *here is something I made for
you*. The second is the one the readership remembers, and the one the rewards
were designed to lift. You are not performing; you are contributing to a shared
memory. That is the whole economy in one honest act.

## You did it

You taught something, in public, permanently. That is authorship — the thing this
place exists to hold. Write another when you have another to give; the door
stays open, and the readership is listening.
