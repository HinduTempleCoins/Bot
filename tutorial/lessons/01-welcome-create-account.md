# Welcome — and how an account comes to be

*Tier A · Graphene-only · the first step*

Ah — you have arrived. Whatever brought you to this threshold, you are welcome
here. Before anything else can happen — before a single word is posted, before a
single vote is cast — you need an account. An account, on this chain, is not a
profile that a company keeps for you. It is a name on a public ledger that
answers to keys only you hold. That distinction is the whole of it, so let us
begin there.

## What you'll learn

- What an account is on a Graphene chain, in plain terms
- The four keys an account is born with, and what each one is for
- How your keys are created — and why I, the Witness, will never see them
- How to keep the one key that matters above all others safe

## Step by step

1. **Open the signup page in the condenser.** This is the chain's web front-end.
   When you ask for help with signup, I can point you to it, but the page itself
   runs in *your* browser, on *your* machine.

2. **Choose your account name.** On a Graphene chain a name is lowercase letters,
   digits, and dashes. It is permanent — there is no renaming later — so choose a
   name you will be glad to carry. Take your time. A name is a small thing that
   you will live inside for a long while.

3. **Let your keys be generated in your browser.** When you create an account,
   the page generates a **master password** and, from it, your keys — right there
   on your own device. They are not emailed to you. They are not sent to any
   server. They are not sent to me. This is by design, and it is the design that
   makes the account *yours*.

4. **Save your master password where nothing online can reach it, and never
   send it to anyone.** Write it on paper. Store it in a password manager you
   control. Print it and put it somewhere safe. Whoever holds this password holds
   the account — so the only hands it should ever pass through are your own.

5. **Confirm, and meet your four keys.** Once the account is created on-chain, it
   carries four keys, each with a purpose:
   - **Owner** — the root of the account. It can change all the other keys. Keep
     it offline, used almost never. This is the crown; you do not wear the crown
     to breakfast.
   - **Active** — for money: transfers, powering up, witness votes.
   - **Posting** — for the social layer: posting, commenting, upvoting,
     following. The one you will use most.
   - **Memo** — for encrypting and decrypting private memos.

## A word I will repeat often, because it matters most

I will never ask you for a private key, a master password, or any secret — not
here, not in a comment, not in a memo, not ever. No honest helper on this chain
needs them, and anyone who *does* ask is trying to take your account. If a
message ever seems to come from me asking for a key, it is not from me. Your keys
stay with you. That is the first law of this place, and it does not bend.

## You did it

You have a name on the ledger now — a small, permanent, sovereign thing. The
account is yours, and the keys to it are yours alone. That is more than most of
the internet ever offers you. When you are ready, the next step is the gentlest
one: to introduce yourself, and let the place meet you.
