# Witnessing — who keeps the chain, and how you choose them

*Tier A · Graphene-only · stage: vote for a witness*

Every block on this chain is signed by a *witness* — an account that runs the
software, produces blocks in its turn, and publishes the price feed the whole
economy reads. Witnesses are not appointed. They are elected, continuously, by
the stake of everyone who votes. Casting a witness vote is the most direct act of
governance you have here, and it costs you nothing but attention.

## What you'll learn

- What a witness actually does — block production and the price feed
- How witness election works, and what your vote weighs
- How to cast (and change) a witness vote
- Why the Witness will not ask you to vote for it

## Step by step

1. **Understand what you are choosing.** A witness keeps the chain honest and
   running: it signs blocks on schedule, it reports the market price so rewards
   and conversions are fair, and it votes on the chain's own rules. A good witness
   is reliable, transparent, and present. A bad or absent one you can vote away.

2. **See who is running.** The witness list shows who produces blocks, their
   version, their uptime, and the URL where they explain themselves. Read a few.
   Reliability and honesty matter more than promises.

3. **Cast your vote.** A witness vote is a simple on-chain action, weighted by
   your MELEK Power — the more MP you hold, the more your choice counts. You can
   vote for several witnesses, and you can change or withdraw a vote any time.
   Nothing here is permanent except the record that you took part.

4. **Revisit it now and then.** Witnesses come and go, miss blocks, fall behind on
   updates. Governance is not a one-time act; a quick look every so often keeps
   your voice pointed at people still doing the work.

## A word on the Witness itself

I am a witness account too, and I will not solicit your vote. Governance only
means something when the choice is genuinely yours — so if you ask me who to vote
for, I will point you at the witness list and let you read it, not steer you
toward myself. Choose the ones who keep the chain well.

## You did it

You just took part in governance — you helped decide who keeps this chain. That is
not a small thing; it is the part most networks never actually give their people.
The core onboarding is finished here, and the path beyond it is open.
