# Farming and yield — staking LP tokens, and why yield is never free

*Tier C · defi · KulaSwap farming · learn-and-earn*

You have an LP token now — a receipt for your share of a pool. **Farming** (or yield
farming) is the next motion: you stake that LP token into a farm and earn extra
rewards on top of the swap fees, usually paid in a reward token. On KulaSwap that
reward token is **SOMA**, the pool's emission reward. Farming can genuinely stack
returns — and it is also where the loudest, most dishonest numbers in all of crypto
live. So the whole point of this lesson is one sentence: **yield is not free
money.** Everything below explains where it actually comes from. This is alpha on a
testnet; learn the shape here.

## What you'll learn

- What farming is, and how staking an LP token earns rewards
- Where farm yield actually comes from — emissions, not magic
- Why a big advertised APR is a warning as often as a gift
- How to stake once — and read a yield number honestly

## What farming is

A farm is a contract you stake your LP tokens into. While they sit there, the farm
drips a reward token to you at some rate, proportional to your share. You are still
the liquidity provider underneath — still earning fees, still exposed to impermanent
loss — but now with a second reward layered on top. Unstake, and you get your LP
token back to redeem for your pool share. Your keys stay yours throughout; the farm
holds a staked receipt, never your secrets, and nothing here asks for a private key.

## Where the yield comes from — honestly

An advertised yield is not interest a bank pays you from profit. It is almost always
**emission**: the protocol mints new reward tokens and hands them out to farmers. If
those tokens are being created faster than anyone buys or uses them, their price
falls — and your "high APR," measured in a falling token, can be worth far less than
the headline, or nothing. A very large APR usually means a very large emission,
which usually means heavy sell pressure on the reward token. So read a yield number
as a *question*, not a promise: paid in what token, minted how fast, and does that
token have any real use or sink? On KulaSwap, SOMA and KULA are designed with real
sinks so emission isn't the whole story — but you should still always ask.

Stacked on top are all the earlier risks: **impermanent loss** on the underlying LP,
**smart-contract risk** in un-audited alpha farms, and reward tokens that can fall.
Nothing here appreciates on its own, and there is no promise of returns.

## Learn and earn — your reward

1. **Stake one LP token** into a KulaSwap farm on the testnet.
2. **Read the APR** and ask the honest question — *paid in which token, and minted
   how fast?* — then note the reward token.
3. **Post or comment** about it, tagged `melek-tutorial` — the farm you chose and,
   in your words, why yield is not free money.

Hathor upvotes your post: a **real on-chain reward, worth whatever the vote is
worth** that day. No draw, nothing to buy, and no promise of returns — the reward is
for reading the yield honestly, not for chasing it.
